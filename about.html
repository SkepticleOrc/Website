🔹 My Journey

My name is Chris, and at 35 years old, I am completely redefining my career path. Growing up, my aspirations were heavily shaped by my environment and early childhood trauma. I wanted to be a police officer—a desire to protect and serve that eventually led me into high-pressure, intense roles within the Security and Prison Services. Looking back, I realized that my deep drive to protect and help others was rooted in what I completely lacked growing up: a true sense of security. For a long time, I believed this defensive path was my definitive calling. However, as the years passed, I came to understand that those roles were a reaction to my past, rather than a reflection of what I genuinely love doing or who I want to be. I needed a complete shift—away from the high-stress environments of public service and toward a career rooted in creation, logic, and problem-solving.

🔹 Notepad to Scripting

While my day jobs were intense, a quiet curiosity for technology always remained in the background. My history with coding actually started 19 years ago when I was 16. I vividly remembered sitting at an old PC, opening up the basic Windows Notepad, and trying to build a primitive website using raw HTML. At that age, I didn't understand coding at all, and I certainly had no idea what CSS or JavaScript even were—but the initial spark was planted.

Over the nearly two decades that followed, I dabbled in and out of code as a casual side hobby. It was never a continuous, daily grind; rather, it was a quiet space I would return to over the years to experiment, mod video games, and look under the hood of digital systems entirely on my own terms. A major milestone of this hobby phase came in 2022, when I pushed myself to build and launch a complete, functional obstacle course (obby) game on Roblox under my long-term online handle, SkepticleOrc.

🔹 The Turning Point

It is only within the last few months that everything has truly crystallized. I returned to my 2022 Roblox project with fresh eyes and refactored the codebase to implement Roblox’s modern CollectionService tag system. Streamlining that logic to make the game mechanics scalable made something click. Looking back, I only wish I had decided to pursue technology sooner—but that realization has given me an immense amount of drive.

To be completely transparent, while my timeline spans 19 years of casual dabbling, I still have an immense amount to learn. I am not an expert, and I refuse to let that long history mask the fact that I am still building my foundational skills. I know I have significant knowledge gaps, and that is precisely why I am pursuing formal education. I am no longer content to just tinker in and out of code as a temporary escape; I am taking my scattered foundations and fully committing to turning them into a verified, professional skillset.

🔹 What Drives Me

What truly drew me into this field all those years ago, and what keeps me here today, is how coding makes me feel. There is a deep, genuine sense of fulfillment in sitting down with a complex problem, writing out logic, and watching your creations come to life. Coding provides an incredible flow state for me—it is an environment where I can easily get completely lost in the zone for hours at a time, entirely focused on building, debugging, and refining.

Because I find so much happiness in this process, I refuse to cut corners or copy tutorials blindly. If a piece of code sits on my page—whether it is a complex layout breakpoint or an interactive script—I make sure I completely understand how it operates under the hood before moving forward. For me, coding isn't just a career switch; it is a genuine passion where hours disappear because I love the challenge of making things work and scaling software logic.

🔹 The Next Chapter

For as long as I can remember, I have always wanted to earn a university degree. It was a massive personal goal of mine, but until this point in my life, I never fully knew what I wanted it to be in. I didn't want to pursue higher education just for the sake of a piece of paper; I wanted it to be for something I was truly driven by. Finding my direction in programming changed everything, giving me the clear focus I had been waiting for.

To back up my practical portfolio work with a flawless academic foundation, I am officially starting my Q62 Computing & IT degree (Software Route) with the Open University this October.

I am going into this university journey with one definitive goal: to work my ass off and earn a First-Class Honours degree. I know that a degree won't teach me everything, and I know I will still have massive things to learn when I graduate, but I am entirely committed to putting in the grueling hours required to reach the top tier of my class. Right now, I am spending the months leading up to my first term independently mastering layout architecture, responsive development, and fluid device breakpoints. Getting ahead of the curve today ensures I build a rock-solid coding foundation, hitting the ground running this autumn as a dedicated, disciplined, and relentlessly focused software engineering student.
